The WFM glossary

Operational terms, practical definitions and common formulas for contact-centre planning. Treat each formula as a starting point: confirm your organisation's metric definitions, denominator rules, time windows and reporting source before using it in production.

30 terms

Demand and forecasting

Demand

Offered contacts

Contacts presented to the operation during a defined period, according to the source system's counting rules.

Illustrative voice model: Offered = Handled + Abandons

Confirm treatment of short abandons, transfers, callbacks and contacts excluded by business rules.

Demand

Forecast

An estimate of future contact volume, workload or staffing need for a defined horizon and interval.

Forecasts should document source data, horizon, granularity, assumptions, version and owner.

Demand

Forecast error

The difference between actual demand and forecast demand. Always state the sign convention used.

Error = Actual − Forecast

Demand

WAPE

Weighted Absolute Percentage Error measures total absolute error relative to total actual volume. It is often useful when intervals have different volumes.

WAPE = Σ|Actual − Forecast| ÷ ΣActual × 100%

Define the evaluation window and handle zero-total actuals explicitly.

Demand

Forecast bias

A persistent tendency to over-forecast or under-forecast. The sign depends on the chosen error convention.

Mean error = average(Actual − Forecast)

Demand

Seasonality

A recurring demand pattern tied to time, such as day of week, month, holiday or predictable business cycle.

Separate repeatable seasonality from one-off events and structural changes.

Service and contact metrics

Service

Service level (SL)

The proportion of eligible offered contacts answered within a defined threshold. Abandon and exclusion rules differ by operation.

SL = eligible answers within threshold ÷ eligible offered contacts

Service

ASA · Average Speed of Answer

Average time eligible contacts wait before being answered, using the source system's defined answer and exclusion rules.

ASA = total eligible wait time ÷ eligible answered contacts

Do not assume the denominator includes abandoned contacts.

Service

AHT · Average Handle Time

Average agent handling time per handled contact. It commonly combines talk, hold and after-contact work, but definitions vary.

AHT = (talk + hold + ACW time) ÷ handled contacts

Service

Abandon rate

The share of eligible offered contacts abandoned before answer under the reporting policy.

Abandon rate = eligible abandons ÷ eligible offered contacts

Service

Handled contacts

Contacts processed by an agent or automated handling path and counted as handled by the source system. Transfers and repeat segments can affect counts.

Service

ACW · After-Contact Work

Post-contact work such as notes, disposition codes or case updates. ACW may be included in AHT when the metric definition says so.

Service

Threshold

The time limit used in a service-level target, such as answering within a specified number of seconds.

Always report the threshold alongside the percentage target.

Service

Interval

A defined time bucket used for reporting or planning, such as 15, 30 or 60 minutes. Interval length changes workload and staffing interpretation.

Workload and capacity

Capacity

Workload

Expected handling effort required by demand in a period.

Workload hours = contacts × AHT seconds ÷ 3,600

This is offered workload arithmetic, not by itself a staffing answer.

Capacity

Erlang

A unit of traffic intensity. One Erlang represents one unit of continuous busy traffic over the measured period.

Offered load (Erlangs) = arrival rate per second × mean handling seconds

Capacity

Erlang C

A queueing model used to estimate waiting and staffing for a simplified multi-agent queue. It assumes, among other things, Poisson arrivals, exponential service times and no abandonment.

Real operations may violate these assumptions; validate model outputs against observed performance.

Capacity

Occupancy

The share of eligible available time spent handling contacts, according to the platform's metric definitions.

Illustrative: handling time ÷ (handling time + eligible idle time)

Do not confuse occupancy with schedule adherence or utilisation.

Capacity

Concurrency

The number of simultaneous interactions an agent can handle in a channel such as chat. Effective capacity depends on interaction mix, complexity and policy.

Capacity

Capacity plan

A model connecting forecast demand, workload, productive hours, skill constraints, shrinkage and service objectives to required capacity.

Capacity

Net staffing

Agents available to perform the required work after relevant availability adjustments. The precise definition must state whether breaks, shrinkage and non-queue activities are already deducted.

Capacity

Multi-channel planning

Planning capacity across channels with different arrival patterns, response expectations, handling times, concurrency and priority rules.

People, schedules and operational control

People

Shrinkage

Paid or scheduled time unavailable for handling the workload being planned, for reasons such as breaks, training, meetings or absence, according to the local definition.

Required scheduled staff ≈ net required staff ÷ (1 − shrinkage fraction)

Use this only when shrinkage is expressed as a fraction of scheduled time and has not already been included elsewhere.

People

Schedule adherence

How closely observed activities align with the published schedule, using the organisation's tolerance and activity rules.

People

Schedule efficiency

A measure of how well scheduled coverage aligns with required demand. Definitions vary; document the exact calculation before comparing teams.

People

RTA · Real-Time Adherence

Monitoring whether agent activities match the planned schedule and helping operations respond to coverage risks as they emerge.

People

Intraday management

Operational decisions made as actual demand and staffing differ from plan, such as schedule adjustments, activity moves or escalation.

People

Skill / routing group

A set of contacts and agents grouped by routing eligibility or capability. Skill mapping should be consistent across forecast, schedule and actual reporting.

People

Coverage gap

The difference between required and available capacity for a defined interval, skill and channel. State the unit: agents, hours, workload or another measure.

People

Service risk

A forward-looking indication that available capacity, demand or operational constraints may cause a target to be missed. State the evidence and confidence behind the alert.

Formula governance: These are teaching definitions, not universal system specifications. Before operational use, reconcile each metric with the source platform, business policy and reporting contract. Never mix cumulative start-of-day snapshots with interval metrics as if they were equivalent.
