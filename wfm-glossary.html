The WFM glossary

Operational terms, practical definitions and common formulas for contact-centre planning. Treat each formula as a starting point: confirm your organisation's metric definitions, denominator rules, time windows and reporting source before using it in production.

Glossary terms

All terms · A to Z

Service

Abandon rate

The share of eligible offered contacts abandoned before answer under the reporting policy.

Abandon rate = eligible abandons ÷ eligible offered contacts

Service

ACD · Automatic Call Distributor

A system that routes incoming calls to queues, skills or agents according to configured rules. Routing rules influence the data WFM uses.

Service

ACW · After-Contact Work

Post-contact work such as notes, disposition codes or case updates. ACW may be included in AHT when the metric definition says so.

Service

AHT · Average Handle Time

Average agent handling time per handled contact. It commonly combines talk, hold and after-contact work, but definitions vary.

AHT = (talk + hold + ACW time) ÷ handled contacts

Service

ASA · Average Speed of Answer

Average time eligible contacts wait before being answered, using the source system's defined answer and exclusion rules.

ASA = total eligible wait time ÷ eligible answered contacts

Do not assume the denominator includes abandoned contacts.

Service

Blended agent

An agent who handles more than one work type, such as inbound and outbound calls, or multiple channels.

Capacity must be assessed across competing work types, priorities and available time, not by adding each channel's full capacity independently.

Service

BPO · Business Process Outsourcing

An arrangement where a provider performs defined business processes for another organisation. WFM may operate against contractual service levels and commercial constraints.

Capacity

Capacity plan

A model connecting forecast demand, workload, productive hours, skill constraints, shrinkage and service objectives to required capacity.

Capacity

Concurrency

The number of simultaneous interactions an agent can handle in a channel such as chat. Effective capacity depends on interaction mix, complexity and policy.

People

Coverage gap

The difference between required and available capacity for a defined interval, skill and channel. State the unit: agents, hours, workload or another measure.

Capacity

Erlang

A unit of traffic intensity. One Erlang represents one unit of continuous busy traffic over the measured period.

Offered load (Erlangs) = arrival rate per second × mean handling seconds

Capacity

Erlang A

A queueing model that includes customer patience and abandonment, unlike classic Erlang C. Its results depend on the assumed arrival, service and patience distributions.

Offered load (Erlangs) = arrival rate per second × mean handling time in seconds

The formula gives offered load, not the final agent requirement. Erlang A requires a queueing calculation.

Capacity

Erlang B

A loss model commonly used to estimate blocked traffic or trunk capacity when arrivals finding all circuits busy are cleared rather than queued.

Offered traffic (Erlangs) = calls per hour × mean holding time in seconds ÷ 3,600

Use the Erlang B model for circuit blocking, not as a substitute for queued-agent staffing.

Capacity

Erlang C

A queueing model used to estimate waiting and staffing for a simplified multi-agent queue. It assumes, among other things, Poisson arrivals, exponential service times and no abandonment.

Real operations may violate these assumptions; validate model outputs against observed performance.

Service

FCR · First Contact Resolution

The proportion of eligible customer issues resolved in the first contact, under the organisation's definition of a repeat or unresolved issue.

FCR = eligible issues resolved at first contact ÷ eligible issues × 100%

Define the repeat-contact window and what counts as resolution.

Demand

Forecast

An estimate of future contact volume, workload or staffing need for a defined horizon and interval.

Forecasts should document source data, horizon, granularity, assumptions, version and owner.

Demand

Forecast bias

A persistent tendency to over-forecast or under-forecast. The sign depends on the chosen error convention.

Mean error = average(Actual − Forecast)

Demand

Forecast error

The difference between actual demand and forecast demand. Always state the sign convention used.

Error = Actual − Forecast

Demand

Forecast interval profile

The distribution of forecast demand across intervals within a day or week.

Interval share = interval volume ÷ total volume for the profile window

Shares should sum to 100% over the same complete window and scope.

Service

Handled contacts

Contacts processed by an agent or automated handling path and counted as handled by the source system. Transfers and repeat segments can affect counts.

Demand

Index factor

A multiplier used to adjust a baseline for a known difference, such as a seasonal or business change.

Adjusted forecast = baseline forecast × index factor

Example: 1,000 contacts × 1.08 = 1,080 forecast contacts.

Service

Interval

A defined time bucket used for reporting or planning, such as 15, 30 or 60 minutes. Interval length changes workload and staffing interpretation.

Arrival rate per second = interval contacts ÷ interval duration in seconds

People

Intraday management

Operational decisions made as actual demand and staffing differ from plan, such as schedule adjustments, activity moves or escalation.

Service

IVR · Interactive Voice Response

A self-service phone system that lets callers navigate menus or complete tasks before or without reaching an agent.

Agent-offered contacts may differ from total inbound contacts because of IVR containment and routing rules.

Capacity

Multi-channel planning

Planning capacity across channels with different arrival patterns, response expectations, handling times, concurrency and priority rules.

Email workload hours = email volume × average work minutes ÷ 60

Chat concurrency and asynchronous work need separate assumptions. Do not apply voice Erlang outputs directly to every channel.

Capacity

Net staffing

Agents available to perform the required work after relevant availability adjustments. The precise definition must state whether breaks, shrinkage and non-queue activities are already deducted.

Capacity

Occupancy

The share of eligible available time spent handling contacts, according to the platform's metric definitions.

Illustrative: handling time ÷ (handling time + eligible idle time)

Do not confuse occupancy with schedule adherence or utilisation.

People

Occupancy versus adherence

Occupancy describes how available agent time is spent handling work; adherence compares observed activities with the planned schedule. They answer different questions.

Adherence % = adherent eligible time ÷ total eligible scheduled time × 100%

Use the platform's tolerance, activity mapping and denominator rules.

Demand

Offered contacts

Contacts presented to the operation during a defined period, according to the source system's counting rules.

Illustrative voice model: Offered = Handled + Abandons

Confirm treatment of short abandons, transfers, callbacks and contacts excluded by business rules.

Demand

Peaked call arrival

A short surge in arrivals beyond the expected pattern. Daily totals can hide these interval-level peaks.

Interval workload (hours) = interval volume × AHT seconds ÷ 3,600

Capacity

Poisson arrivals

A statistical arrival model often used in queueing theory. It assumes arrivals occur independently at a stable average rate over the modelled period.

For rate λ over duration t: P(N = k) = e^(−λt)(λt)^k ÷ k!

Real arrival bursts, campaigns and correlated demand can violate this assumption.

Capacity

Pooling principle

Combining eligible demand and resources can reduce variability and improve efficiency when skills, routing and service objectives allow agents to share work.

Pooling is not automatic: language, client, regulatory, priority and skill restrictions can limit shared capacity.

Demand

Quantitative forecasting

Forecasting that uses historical data and statistical methods, including time-series and explanatory approaches.

Simple baseline: Forecast = same weekday's comparable historical average

This is a baseline method, not a universal best model. Test against holdout periods.

Service

Queue

A waiting line or logical holding area for contacts when immediate service is unavailable. Queue design and routing priorities affect wait times.

Queue service metrics should specify queue, skill, interval and eligible contact population.

People

RAG · Red, Amber, Green

A status convention for communicating performance or risk. Each colour should map to explicit thresholds, data freshness rules and operational actions.

Example: status = f(actual KPI, target, tolerance, data freshness)

There is no universal RAG threshold. Use approved local rules and avoid inferring risk from stale or cumulative data alone.

People

RSF · Rostered Staff Factor

An uplift factor used to translate base/net staffing into rostered staffing, accounting for shrinkage when the organisation's definitions support this calculation.

RSF = 1 ÷ (1 − shrinkage fraction)

Rostered staff = net staff × RSF. This is mathematically equivalent to dividing net staff by (1 − shrinkage), not adding the shrinkage percentage directly.

People

RTA · Real-Time Adherence

Monitoring whether agent activities match the planned schedule and helping operations respond to coverage risks as they emerge.

People

Schedule adherence

How closely observed activities align with the published schedule, using the organisation's tolerance and activity rules.

People

Schedule efficiency

A measure of how well scheduled coverage aligns with required demand. Definitions vary; document the exact calculation before comparing teams.

Demand

Seasonality

A recurring demand pattern tied to time, such as day of week, month, holiday or predictable business cycle.

Separate repeatable seasonality from one-off events and structural changes.

Service

Service level (SL)

The proportion of eligible offered contacts answered within a defined threshold. Abandon and exclusion rules differ by operation.

SL = eligible answers within threshold ÷ eligible offered contacts

People

Service risk

A forward-looking indication that available capacity, demand or operational constraints may cause a target to be missed. State the evidence and confidence behind the alert.

People

Shrinkage

Paid or scheduled time unavailable for handling the workload being planned, for reasons such as breaks, training, meetings or absence, according to the local definition.

Required scheduled staff ≈ net required staff ÷ (1 − shrinkage fraction)

Use this only when shrinkage is expressed as a fraction of scheduled time and has not already been included elsewhere.

People

Skill / routing group

A set of contacts and agents grouped by routing eligibility or capability. Skill mapping should be consistent across forecast, schedule and actual reporting.

Service

Threshold

The time limit used in a service-level target, such as answering within a specified number of seconds.

Always report the threshold alongside the percentage target.

Capacity

True calls per hour

A historical productivity concept sometimes used to compare output with occupancy. Definitions vary, so document the exact measure before using it.

Illustrative definition: calls handled ÷ occupancy hours

Do not divide by an occupancy percentage without converting it into a time basis consistent with the numerator.

Demand

WAPE

Weighted Absolute Percentage Error measures total absolute error relative to total actual volume. It is often useful when intervals have different volumes.

WAPE = Σ|Actual − Forecast| ÷ ΣActual × 100%

Define the evaluation window and handle zero-total actuals explicitly.

People

Waterfall schedule

A schedule pattern where planned hours or staffing vary by day to reflect changing demand through the week.

Coverage gap = required staff − available staff

Negative values indicate surplus against the defined requirement; positive values indicate a shortfall.

People

WEM · Workforce Engagement Management

A technology and management category that can combine workforce tools with quality, coaching, performance and employee engagement capabilities. Vendor definitions vary.

People

WFM · Workforce Management

The discipline of forecasting demand, translating it into staffing requirements, scheduling people and monitoring adherence and intraday performance.

WFM planning chain: forecast demand → workload/staffing → schedules → intraday control → review

People

WFO · Workforce Optimisation

A broader operational approach that may combine WFM with quality, performance, analytics and other tools. Exact product scope varies by organisation and vendor.

Capacity

Workload

Expected handling effort required by demand in a period.

Workload hours = contacts × AHT seconds ÷ 3,600

This is offered workload arithmetic, not by itself a staffing answer.

People

Wrap-up code / disposition code

A label agents use to classify the outcome or reason for an interaction. Consistent codes support contact-reason analysis and forecasting drivers.

Code share = contacts with selected code ÷ eligible coded contacts × 100%

Define treatment of missing, multi-coded and invalid records.

Source and formula governance: This independent glossary is informed by Call Centre Helper's Contact Centre Jargon Glossary and other specialist references. Definitions are paraphrased and curated for WFM learning; the source's article text and assets are not reproduced. Formulas are included where the concept is mathematically defined, with assumptions and denominator cautions. Before operational use, reconcile each metric with the source platform, business policy and reporting contract. Never mix cumulative start-of-day snapshots with interval metrics as if they were equivalent.
