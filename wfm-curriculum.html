Read the operation through its KPIs

Learn to read a contact-centre snapshot without confusing volume, outcomes and service performance.

1. Start with the operating story

WFM decisions begin with trustworthy definitions. A KPI is useful only when you know what it counts, the period it covers, its denominator and the operational decision it informs. Never compare two percentages until you confirm they were calculated on the same basis.

  • Offered: contacts entering the defined queue or reporting scope.
  • Handled: contacts answered/handled by agents under the source system's definition.
  • Abandoned: contacts that leave before an agent answers, under the configured reporting rules.
  • AHT: average handling time per handled contact, based on the source's included talk, hold and after-contact work components.
  • ASA: average queue wait for contacts that were answered, according to the source definition.
  • Service level: the share meeting a stated answer-time threshold. The denominator depends on the reporting configuration, so verify it.

2. Worked example: inspect the snapshot

Assume this simplified interval uses the agreed rule Offered = Handled + Abandons and includes abandons in the service-level denominator.

MeasureObserved valueWhat to check
Handled920Answered/handled contacts
Abandoned80Confirm exclusions such as short abandons
Offered1,000920 + 80 reconciles
Answered within threshold736Confirm threshold, e.g. 20 seconds
AHT300 secondsConfirm included handling components
ASA35 secondsConfirm eligible answered-contact denominator
Service level = Answered within threshold ÷ Offered × 100
736 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 73.6%

If someone instead divides 736 by 920 handled contacts, the result is 80%. The arithmetic is valid, but it answers a different question and does not match the agreed denominator. The operational lesson is not “one formula always fits every platform”; it is “make the metric definition explicit, then apply it consistently.”

3. Your turn: audit the KPI

The dashboard displays service level as 80%, using 736 ÷ 920, but the agreed rule for this exercise is answered within threshold ÷ offered. What should you do?

4. What you should be able to do

  • Reconcile offered, handled and abandoned contacts when the scope supports that equation.
  • State the service-level threshold and denominator, not just the percentage.
  • Flag mismatches rather than silently editing source data.
  • Keep a metric dictionary with the formula, source, exclusions, reporting grain and owner.
